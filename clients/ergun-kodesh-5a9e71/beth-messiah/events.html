Feasts & Holy Days

Shabbat — שַׁבָּת

Weekly

The seventh-day rest set apart each week to cease from labor and delight in God's presence with His people.

Passover — פֶּסַח

Spring · Nisan

Remembers the exodus from Egypt and the lamb's blood that spared Israel's firstborn, pointing us to Messiah as our Passover Lamb.

Unleavened Bread & Firstfruits — בִּכּוּרִים

Spring · Nisan

A week of clearing leaven from the home as a picture of sin put away, with Firstfruits marking Messiah's resurrection as the first of the harvest.

Shavuot — שָׁבוּעוֹת

Late Spring · Sivan

Celebrates the giving of the Torah at Sinai and, for believers, the outpouring of the Spirit on the early disciples.

Rosh Hashanah — רֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה

Fall · Tishrei

The Feast of Trumpets opens the fall holy days with the sounding of the shofar, calling God's people to awaken and prepare their hearts.

Yom Kippur — יוֹם כִּפּוּר

Fall · Tishrei

The Day of Atonement, the most solemn day of the year, set apart for fasting and repentance while pointing to Messiah's once-for-all atoning sacrifice.

Sukkot — סֻכּוֹת

Fall · Tishrei

The Feast of Tabernacles recalls Israel's wilderness wandering in temporary shelters and looks forward to God tabernacling among His people.

Hanukkah — חֲנֻכָּה

Winter · Kislev

The Festival of Lights commemorates the rededication of the Temple and celebrates God's faithfulness in preserving His people.

Purim — פּוּרִים

Late Winter · Adar

Celebrates the deliverance of the Jewish people in Persia through Esther and Mordecai, a joyful reminder that God protects His people even when He seems hidden.
