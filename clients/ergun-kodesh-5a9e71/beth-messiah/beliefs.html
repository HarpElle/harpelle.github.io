What We Believe

Our Statement of Faith

We believe the Scriptures — the Tanakh (Hebrew Bible) and the Brit Chadashah (New Testament) — are the inspired, authoritative, and reliable word of God, given through human writers under the guidance of the Holy Spirit. Together they form one unified revelation, with the New Testament writings fulfilling and illuminating the promises and Torah of the Old. They remain our final rule for faith, doctrine, and daily living.

2 Tim. 3:16-17; Ps. 119:105; Matt. 5:17-18

We believe every human being is created in the image of God, male and female, and carries inherent dignity and worth. Through Adam's disobedience in the garden, sin entered the world, separating all people from God and leaving humanity unable to restore that relationship on its own. Every person therefore needs the redemption made possible through Messiah Yeshua.

Gen. 1:26-27; Gen. 3:1-19; Rom. 3:23; Rom. 5:12

We believe in one God, eternal, holy, and sovereign, the Creator and Sustainer of heaven and earth. He alone is worthy of worship, unchanging in His character, and intimately involved in the affairs of His creation and of His covenant people Israel. He has revealed Himself throughout Scripture as both transcendent and near.

Deut. 6:4; Gen. 1:1; Isa. 45:5-6; Ps. 90:2

We believe God the Father is the source of all things, who chose Israel as His covenant people and adopts all who trust in Yeshua as His own children, calling Him Abba. Consistent with the Shema's declaration that the LORD is Echad — a united, composite oneness rather than a solitary one — the Father is fully God, distinct in person yet one in essence with the Son and the Spirit. He loves, disciplines, and provides for His children as a faithful Father.

Deut. 6:4; Matt. 6:9; Rom. 8:15; Mal. 2:10

We believe Yeshua (Jesus) is HaBen, the eternal Son, who took on human flesh, lived a sinless life, died as an atoning sacrifice for sin, and rose bodily from the grave. As the Shema's Echad affirms of the one God — a united oneness rather than a solitary one — the Son is fully God and fully man, one in essence with the Father and the Spirit while distinct in person. He now reigns at the Father's right hand and will return to establish His kingdom.

Deut. 6:4; John 1:1,14; Isa. 9:6; Phil. 2:6-11

We believe the Ruach HaKodesh is the Spirit of the living God, sent to indwell, seal, and empower every believer for holy living and service. Consistent with the Echad of the Shema — the one God's united, composite oneness rather than a solitary one — the Spirit is fully God, distinct in person from the Father and the Son yet one with them in essence. He convicts of sin, teaches truth, and produces the fruit and gifts that build up the body of Messiah.

Deut. 6:4; Joel 2:28-29; John 16:13; Gal. 5:22-23

We believe in the bodily resurrection of both the righteous and the unrighteous — the righteous to eternal life with God, and the unrighteous to eternal judgment. Yeshua's own resurrection guarantees this future hope for all who trust in Him, and every person will one day stand before God to give account. This truth shapes how we live now, in holiness and hope.

Dan. 12:2; John 5:28-29; 1 Cor. 15:20-23; Rev. 20:11-15

We believe Yeshua of Nazareth is the promised Messiah of Israel, born of a virgin, a descendant of King David, who fulfilled the Hebrew Scriptures' prophecies concerning the coming Redeemer. He was crucified as the once-for-all sacrifice for sin, was raised bodily on the third day, and ascended to the Father. He alone provides atonement and reconciliation with God for both Jew and Gentile.

Isa. 53:1-12; Mic. 5:2; Luke 24:25-27; John 14:6

We believe God's covenant promises to national and ethnic Israel remain in force, unrevoked and unreplaced by the church. The Scriptures point to a future regathering, restoration, and spiritual awakening of Israel, and to God's continuing faithfulness to the descendants of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The church does not replace Israel but is grafted in alongside her, sharing in the promises made to the patriarchs.

Rom. 11:1-2, 25-29; Jer. 31:35-37; Ezek. 36:24-28; Gen. 12:1-3

We believe Messianic Judaism is a biblically and historically grounded expression of faith for Jewish people who trust in Yeshua as Messiah while retaining their Jewish identity, heritage, and practice. From the earliest days of the Body of Messiah in Jerusalem, Jewish followers of Yeshua continued to observe Torah, keep the feasts, and gather in synagogue life. We welcome Jew and Gentile alike to worship together, honoring this heritage as a gift to the whole Body of Messiah.

Acts 21:20; Rom. 11:17-18; Gal. 3:28-29; Acts 15:1-21
